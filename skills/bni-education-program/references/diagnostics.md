# Diagnosing what the chapter actually needs

BNI Australia's own method, from the *Creating Education Moments* LTT participant guide:
read the Chapter Traffic Lights Report, find the gap, teach to the gap. This file turns
that into a repeatable step.

Do not skip this and pick topics that seem nice. A program that isn't aimed at a gap is
just content.

---

## Input tier 1 — the reports (preferred)

### Chapter Traffic Lights (BNI Connect)

Eight columns. Each metric scores 0 / 5 / 10 / 15, and the total (`Pts`) drives the
row's red / amber / green band. The trailing `+15`, `=`, `-10` is the movement since
last period — **read it**, because a green chapter sliding down needs different
education from an amber chapter climbing.

| Col | Metric | What it really measures | Education can move it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Size` | members | scale | indirectly |
| `Growth` | net members gained | are we replacing what we lose | yes |
| `Ret` | retention % | is membership worth it to people | yes, slowly |
| `Ref` | referrals per member per week | are people actually giving business | **yes, strongly** |
| `Vis` | visitors | is anyone inviting | **yes, strongly** |
| `Conv` | visitor → member conversion % | what happens once they're here | **yes, strongly** |
| `Abs` | absenteeism % | commitment and habit | yes |
| `Pts` | total | overall health | — |

### Member Traffic Lights / Power of One

Five KPIs, 20 points each, 100 total. **70+ is green** (Chapter Operations Manual p111):

- Attendance
- Average referrals given per week
- Average one-to-one meetings per week
- Average visitors per month
- Average Chapter Education Units (CEUs) per week

What matters here is the **distribution**, not the average. Ask: how many green, amber,
red, grey? The Chapter Success Meeting question is "what moves people from yellow to
green, red to yellow, grey to red" (COM p37) — that is exactly what education is for.

> Do not read individual members' scores out to the chapter. These reports are for the
> Leadership Team (COM p111). Education addresses the *pattern*, never the person.
> Recognise the improvers by name; never name the strugglers.

### Accepting the data

Pasted table, CSV, PDF or screenshot are all fine. Read the numbers back to the user
before analysing, so a misread screenshot gets caught early.

## Input tier 2 — the interview

When the reports aren't to hand. Ten questions; the answers map onto the same metrics.

1. How many members are in the chapter right now, and how many were there six months ago?
2. How many visitors came in the last month? How many in a typical month?
3. Of the last ten visitors, how many joined?
4. Roughly how many members bring a visitor in a given month — most, some, or the same two or three?
5. In an average week, how many referrals get passed? Do they come from across the room or the same handful?
6. When a referral is given, is it usually a name and number, or a genuine introduction?
7. How many one-to-ones happen in a typical week?
8. What's attendance like — full room, or a scattering of substitutes and empty chairs?
9. How many members are logging CEUs at all?
10. What has the President or your Director Consultant raised in the last two months?

Also ask: **what has already been tried?** Repeating an education moment the chapter
ignored three months ago will not work better the second time.

## Input tier 3 — context

- Chapter name, meeting day, venue
- Number of weeks in the program, and the start date
- Season, and where in the leadership term you are
- Members who joined in the last three months (new members change the topic mix a lot)
- Anything the chapter is proud of — education should celebrate as well as correct

## Gap → topic mapping

Each red or amber metric points at a slice of the topic banks. Weight the program by
severity: a red metric earns roughly twice the weeks of an amber one.

| Symptom | What's usually really happening | Go to |
|---|---|---|
| `Vis` low | Nobody is inviting, or the same three people are. Members don't know who to invite or feel awkward asking. | `topics-growth.md` |
| `Conv` low with `Vis` healthy | Plenty come, few join. The visitor experience, the follow-up, or the ask is broken. | `topics-growth.md` |
| `Ref` low | Members can't see referrals, don't know each other's businesses, or are passing leads and calling them referrals. | `topics-referrals.md` |
| `Ref` concentrated in a few members | The rest don't know how. This is a skills gap, not a willingness gap. | `topics-referrals.md` |
| `Abs` high | The meeting isn't worth 90 minutes to some people. Substitutes aren't understood. | `topics-engagement.md` |
| `Ret` low | Members reach renewal without having got value. Usually traceable back to one-to-ones never happening. | `topics-engagement.md` |
| CEU KPI red | Members don't know CEUs exist, or don't know they're free and easy. | `topics-engagement.md` |
| One-to-ones low | The single highest-leverage gap in BNI. Fix this and referrals and retention follow. | `topics-engagement.md` |
| `Growth` flat or negative | Losing as many as you gain. Needs both invitation and retention work. | `topics-growth.md` + `topics-engagement.md` |
| Everything green | Do not coast. Go deeper: quality of referrals, TYFCB, mentoring, leadership pipeline. | `topics-referrals.md`, `topics-core-seasonal.md` |
| New members joined recently | They need the basics and the room needs reminding. | `topics-core-seasonal.md` |

### Cause before symptom

Two chapters with identical low `Conv` need different education:

- Visitors leave feeling ignored → the problem is the **room's behaviour**. Teach the
  chapter how to host, and who talks to whom during Open Networking.
- Visitors enjoy it but never hear back → the problem is **follow-up**. Teach the
  seven-day window and who owns the call.
- Visitors are impressed but can't see the money → the problem is the **weekly
  presentation quality** they witnessed. Teach specific asks.

Ask which one it is before choosing. If the user doesn't know, that itself is worth a
week: have the Visitor Host report back on what visitors actually say.

## Writing the gap analysis

Keep it to one page. For each gap:

> **Visitor conversion — 8.6%, red, down 5 points**
> Twenty-three visitors in the last quarter, two joined. Visitors are arriving, so the
> invitation habit is working; the loss is happening after they leave. The Visitor Host
> reports most visitors are not contacted again.
> *Education can move this.* Four weeks aimed at follow-up, the visitor experience, and
> making the ask.

Then say plainly what education **cannot** fix. If the venue is cold and the coffee is
bad, no education moment will lift retention, and pretending otherwise wastes the term.
Name it and pass it to the Leadership Team.

---

## Goals and outcomes

Two to four, no more. Each one needs all five parts:

> **Goal.** Lift visitor conversion from 8.6% to 20%.
> **Why.** Twenty-three visitors produced two members; at 20% that is five.
> **Weeks.** 2, 5, 9, 12.
> **Measured on.** Chapter Traffic Lights, `Conv` column.
> **Reviewed.** March Chapter Success Meeting.

A goal without a measure and a date is a wish. Confirm these with the user before
building the weeks — everything downstream depends on them.
