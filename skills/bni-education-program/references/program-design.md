# Designing the program

Turning a gap analysis into a term of dated weeks that a chapter will actually sit
through and act on.

---

## The arc

A term is not a list of topics. It has a shape.

| Phase | Share of weeks | Job |
|---|---|---|
| **Set up** | first 1–2 weeks | Why we're here, what we're aiming at, what changes. Name the goal out loud. |
| **Work the gap** | the bulk | The red metrics, hardest first while attention is highest. |
| **Reinforce** | interleaved | Return to the biggest gap a second and third time, from a different angle. |
| **Celebrate** | when it moves | The moment a metric shifts, say so and name who did it. |
| **Hand over** | last 1–2 weeks | What we learned, what's next, what the incoming coordinator inherits. |

Two rules that matter more than the phases:

- **Repeat the biggest gap.** One education moment changes almost nothing. Three
  moments on the same gap, spaced four weeks apart and told differently each time,
  changes behaviour. Resist covering more ground.
- **Never run two heavy weeks back to back.** Alternate a corrective topic with a
  warm or celebratory one, or the chapter starts bracing for the education slot.

## Cadence

Over any eight weeks, aim for roughly:

- 4 × primary-gap topics
- 2 × secondary-gap topics
- 1 × core values / Givers Gain / celebration
- 1 × seasonal or chapter-specific

Every week regardless: a visitor beat, an action ask, the ELF close.

## The Australian calendar

The single most common failure is running a US-shaped program in an Australian chapter.
Anchor to what is actually happening outside the room.

| When | What's going on | Education that fits |
|---|---|---|
| **Late Jan – Feb** | Back to business. Highest energy of the year, and the best visitor month. | Goal setting, invitations, the Power of One, "what do you want from BNI this year" |
| **March – April** | Settled and productive. Easter and school holidays scatter attendance. | Referral skills, one-to-ones, substitutes and attendance |
| **May – June** | EOFY. Everyone is busy, distracted and thinking about money. | TYFCB (get it recorded before 30 June), referrals that close, "is BNI working for you" |
| **July** | New financial year, new budgets, fresh intent. Second-best month for invitations. | Reset, invite, new member basics |
| **Aug – Sep** | The grind. Attendance dips, energy flattens, winter. | Motivation, celebration, recognition, why we do this |
| **Oct – Nov** | Strong run home. Businesses want work booked before Christmas. | Urgency, specific asks, referral quality |
| **Dec** | Chapter breaks up, attendance drops, everyone is at end-of-year functions. | Gratitude, celebration, light touch. Nothing corrective. |
| **Jan** | Quiet, many chapters in recess. | Short program, warm re-entry |

Also: leadership terms usually change in **April and October**, so the first and last
weeks of those months carry handover naturally. School holidays vary by state — ask
which state the chapter is in and check the dates rather than assuming.

Never schedule a hard corrective moment for the last meeting before Christmas, or the
first meeting back. Both are wasted.

## Choosing the presenter

The Education Coordinator may assign members to deliver (COM p78), and should. A member
delivering an education moment learns it far better than one listening to it, and the
room hears it differently from a peer.

| Topic type | Best presenter | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Invitations, visitors | someone who actually brings visitors | credibility beats theory |
| Referrals | a member who gives a lot of them | they can describe how they spot one |
| One-to-ones | a member known for good ones | the room already believes them |
| Attendance, policy | Vice President or Membership Committee | it lands as the chapter's voice, not a nag |
| CEUs, learning | Education Coordinator | it's your patch |
| Core values, celebration | a long-standing well-liked member | warmth carries it |
| New member basics | a member who joined 6–12 months ago | they remember being lost |

Practical rules:
- Ask at least three weeks ahead, and send the brief when you ask, not the night before.
- Spread it around. The same three confident members every month teaches the chapter
  that presenting is for confident people.
- A nervous member with a good brief will do fine. That is what the brief is for.
- Keep one deck in reserve that you can deliver cold when someone doesn't show.

## Building the roster

For each week record: week number, date, topic, the one thing, gap served, presenter,
confirmed yes/no. Publish it — a roster nobody can see is a roster nobody honours.
This is the spine of the dashboard.

## The program plan document

`Education-Program-Plan.md` opens with:

1. **Where the chapter is now** — the gap analysis, one page
2. **What we're aiming at** — the 2–4 goals, each with measure and date
3. **How we'll get there** — the week-by-week table
4. **Who's presenting** — the roster
5. **How we'll know** — which report, read when, by whom
6. **What education can't fix** — handed to the Leadership Team

Write it for the President and the Leadership Team as much as for yourself. It is the
document that gets you support for a full term rather than a fortnight.

## Reviewing

Halfway through, read the reports again. If the target metric has not moved at all:

- The diagnosis may be wrong — the cause is somewhere the education isn't aimed.
- The ask may be too vague to act on.
- Or the chapter may need something education cannot give it.

Say so, out loud, and change the back half of the program. A plan that survives contact
with no results is not a plan worth defending.
