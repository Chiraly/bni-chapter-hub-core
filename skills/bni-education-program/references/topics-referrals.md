# Topic bank — referrals, asks, weekly presentations, TYFCB

For chapters with low `Ref`, referrals concentrated in a few members, or plenty of
referrals that never close.

---

### 1. A referral is not a lead
**One thing.** A name and a number is a lead. A referral is an introduction.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Being handed a phone number and being walked over and introduced are two completely different mornings.
**BNI link.** Leads pad the slip count and close at almost nothing. A real referral means you have spoken to the person, they are expecting the call, and they know why.
**Visitor beat.** This is the difference between a networking group and a referral group, and it is the whole reason this room exists.
**Ask.** Before you write a slip this week, ring the person first.
**Presenter.** A member who gives high-quality referrals.
**CEU.** referral lead quality

### 2. Asking for something specific
**One thing.** "Anyone who needs my services" gets you nothing. A named target gets you a referral.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Ask a friend to recommend "a good restaurant" and they stall. Ask for "somewhere for eight people on a Friday in Yarraville" and they answer instantly.
**BNI link.** The room wants to help and cannot see through a vague ask. Specific is not narrow — specific is findable.
**Visitor beat.** Notice what happens when someone asks for something precise. Watch how many pens move.
**Ask.** Write next week's ask now, with a name or a suburb in it.
**Presenter.** A member who asks well.
**CEU.** specific referral ask weekly presentation

### 3. Training the room to see you
**One thing.** Your weekly presentation is not an advert. It is a search instruction.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Nobody remembers a slogan. Everyone remembers a picture of a situation.
**BNI link.** Thirty seconds a week, fifty weeks a year, to teach thirty people what to notice on your behalf. Members who describe a *moment* get referrals; members who describe their *company* do not.
**Visitor beat.** Every business here gets the same thirty seconds. That is the deal.
**Ask.** Rewrite your weekly presentation around one situation, not one service.
**Presenter.** A member who gets referrals from theirs.
**CEU.** weekly presentation referral

### 4. Referrals you are walking past
**One thing.** You already hear referral opportunities every day. You are just not listening for them.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Once you learn a car model you see it everywhere. Attention is trainable.
**BNI link.** Members complain there is no opportunity to refer, then have four conversations a day where someone mentions a problem the room could solve. It is a noticing habit, not a luck problem.
**Visitor beat.** Try it this week in your own business. It costs nothing and it works whether you join or not.
**Ask.** Carry one member's name in your head all week and listen for their moment.
**Presenter.** A high referrer.
**CEU.** referrals see opportunity

### 5. The referral you should not give
**One thing.** A bad referral costs more than no referral.
**Gaps.** Ref, Ret
**Business hook.** Recommend a tradie who does not show up and it is your name that takes the damage, not theirs.
**BNI link.** Slips written to hit a number damage trust in both directions. It is better to give one real referral a month than four manufactured ones.
**Visitor beat.** Nobody here is under quota pressure to send you business you cannot use.
**Ask.** If you have a slip you are not proud of, do not write it. Have a conversation instead.
**Presenter.** Vice President.
**CEU.** referral quality trust

### 6. Say thank you with a number
**One thing.** TYFCB is how the chapter knows it works.
**Gaps.** Ref, Ret
**Business hook.** Any business that cannot say where its revenue came from will cut the wrong cost eventually.
**BNI link.** Unrecorded closed business makes the chapter look weaker than it is, and robs the person who referred you of the credit. It also robs you of the evidence at renewal time.
**Visitor beat.** Ask any member what BNI has been worth to them in dollars. Most can tell you exactly.
**Ask.** Record every piece of closed business from the last month. Today.
**Presenter.** Secretary/Treasurer.
**CEU.** thank you for closed business TYFCB

### 7. Closing the loop
**One thing.** Tell the person who referred you what happened.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Refer a friend to someone and never hear the outcome. You will hesitate before referring them again.
**BNI link.** Feedback is what makes a referrer refer twice. Silence trains the room to stop.
**Visitor beat.** This is a habit worth stealing whether or not you join.
**Ask.** Report back on every referral you have received this month, even the ones that went nowhere.
**Presenter.** Any member.
**CEU.** referral follow up feedback

### 8. Who you actually refer
**One thing.** You refer people you trust, and trust comes from knowing them.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Everyone has a mental shortlist of people they would vouch for. It is short, and it is built on experience, not proximity.
**BNI link.** Members wait to feel confident before referring, and confidence never arrives on its own — it arrives through one-to-ones. If you cannot describe someone's business, you will never refer them.
**Visitor beat.** Nobody here refers a stranger. That is why the coffee before the meeting matters.
**Ask.** Pick the member whose business you understand least and book a one-to-one.
**Presenter.** A member with strong referral partners.
**CEU.** one to one trust referral

### 9. The referral partner
**One thing.** A handful of deep relationships beats thirty shallow ones.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Most businesses get most of their work from a small number of sources. The long tail is real but it is not the engine.
**BNI link.** Find the two or three members whose clients are your clients at a different moment, and work those relationships hard.
**Visitor beat.** Have a look around and think about whose customers are yours six months earlier.
**Ask.** Name your top referral partner. Book a proper session with them.
**Presenter.** A member with an obvious partnership.
**CEU.** referral partner power team contact sphere

### 10. Asking is not begging
**One thing.** Asking clearly is a service to the room, not an imposition on it.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Under-asking is the most common and most expensive habit in small business.
**BNI link.** Members go vague to avoid seeming pushy, and the room cannot act. Vagueness is not politeness — it is just harder to help.
**Visitor beat.** You will hear people ask directly this morning. Notice that nobody minds.
**Ask.** Make one direct ask this week, out loud, of a person.
**Presenter.** A confident member.
**CEU.** ask referral specific

### 11. The one-to-one that produces business
**One thing.** A good one-to-one ends with something to do.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Meetings that end without a next step were a chat, and chats do not appear in revenue.
**BNI link.** GAINS — goals, accomplishments, interests, networks, skills. The last two are where referrals come from and the first two are how you get there.
**Visitor beat.** This is a coffee with a structure, not a sales meeting.
**Ask.** Finish your next one-to-one by naming one person you will each introduce.
**Presenter.** A member known for good one-to-ones.
**CEU.** one to one GAINS

### 12. Referrals in a quiet month
**One thing.** When your own pipeline is slow, give more, not less.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** The instinct when work dries up is to pull inward. It is almost always the wrong move.
**BNI link.** Givers Gain is not sentiment — it is sequencing. The members who give through the quiet months are the ones with a full diary when it turns.
**Visitor beat.** Ask a member what happened in their worst quarter. Most will tell you the room carried them.
**Ask.** Give one referral this week regardless of what your own month looks like.
**Presenter.** A long-standing member.
**CEU.** givers gain referral

### 13. Before 30 June
**One thing.** Get the closed business recorded before the year ends.
**Gaps.** Ref
**Business hook.** Every Australian business spends June tidying up. This is one more thing on the same list, and it takes five minutes.
**BNI link.** TYFCB recorded this financial year is the number you take to your renewal conversation and the number the chapter reports.
**Visitor beat.** Members can tell you what this room returned this financial year. Ask one.
**Ask.** Log every outstanding TYFCB before the end of the month.
**Presenter.** Secretary/Treasurer.
**CEU.** TYFCB closed business
**Season.** May–June only.
